# Nobile Rim 2

Nobile Rim 2 is one of NASA's nine Artemis III candidate landing regions near the Moon's south pole. Another stretch of the rim of Nobile crater, farther from the pole than Nobile Rim 1.

## Where it is

- **Size:** about 400 km².
- **Reference point** (its sunniest pixel on NASA's sunlight map): 83.74°S, 59.40°E, about 190 km from the pole.
- **Sunlight at the reference point:** 54% on NASA's long-term average map (120 m pixels).
- **Landing zones that pass the slope rule:** 31% of those tried (at least 95% of a landing zone of 100 m radius under 10°).

## The #1 landing target (year-round crewed base, 2027)

The target that needs the lightest power kit. The site card in the app shows it. By kit weight it ranks 9 of the 9 regions (1 is lightest).

- **Where:** 84.09°S, 59.62°E, 10.9 km from the reference point.
- **Landing zone:** 100% under 10°; average slope 3.9°, steepest pixel 6.5°.
- **Panel spot:** 128 m from the target, slope 0.1°, on a 10 m mast.
- **Sunlight at the panels:** 52% of the year; longest night 15.2 days (365 hours).
- **At the landing point itself:** 52% sunlight, longest night 15.1 days.
- **Power kit:** 36.0 t: battery 35.6 t (5,339 kWh), solar array 0.4 t (98 m²).
- **Direct Earth contact:** 63% at the target (62%–64% across the zone); longest direct blackout 252 hours (10.5 days) at the worst point in the zone.
- **With the two relays:** about 98% from every point in the zone; longest gap 2 hours.

## Against NASA's sunlight map

The #1 target is number 4 of the ten on NASA's average-sunlight map. The target that map rates best needs 36.6 t, so ranking by kit weight saves 0.6 t.
Across the region's ten targets the kit runs from 36.0 t to 39.8 t.

## Water ice nearby (information only)

The nearest permanently shadowed area of at least 1 km² is 6.6 km from the reference point (3.6 km²). At its hottest it reaches 235 K; 28% of it is cold enough for ice at the surface. It is too far to walk (the on-foot reach is 300 m), and it never changes the kit or a ranking.

## What stands out

- The heaviest power kit of the nine (36.0 t).
- The longest night of the nine at its panels (15.2 days), which is why its battery is so big.
- The region only fits NASA's coarser 120 m sunlight map, so its reference point is less precise.

## Limits

- Numbers are for one case: the crewed mission's year-round base over 2027, panels and antenna at 10 m. Other dates and stays come from the app's own runs.
- Slope comes from 20 m pixels, so boulders and small craters are invisible.
- The ten targets are shortlisted by NASA's average-sunlight map before the full calculation, so a target with a shorter night but lower average could be missed.
