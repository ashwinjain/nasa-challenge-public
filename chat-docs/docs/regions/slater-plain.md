# Slater Plain

Slater Plain is one of NASA's nine Artemis III candidate landing regions near the Moon's south pole. A plain named after the nearby crater Slater.

## Where it is

- **Size:** about 400 km².
- **Reference point** (its sunniest pixel on NASA's sunlight map): 87.27°S, 127.61°E, about 83 km from the pole.
- **Sunlight at the reference point:** 66% on NASA's long-term average map (60 m pixels).
- **Landing zones that pass the slope rule:** 43% of those tried (at least 95% of a landing zone of 100 m radius under 10°).

## The #1 landing target (year-round crewed base, 2027)

The target that needs the lightest power kit. The site card in the app shows it. By kit weight it ranks 8 of the 9 regions (1 is lightest).

- **Where:** 87.23°S, 127.27°E, 1.2 km from the reference point.
- **Landing zone:** 100% under 10°; average slope 1.2°, steepest pixel 5.4°.
- **Panel spot:** 197 m from the target, slope 2.2°, on a 10 m mast.
- **Sunlight at the panels:** 66% of the year; longest night 14.3 days (344 hours).
- **At the landing point itself:** 67% sunlight, longest night 14.3 days.
- **Power kit:** 33.2 t: battery 32.9 t (4,928 kWh), solar array 0.3 t (92 m²).
- **Direct Earth contact:** 47% at the target (47%–47% across the zone); longest direct blackout 343 hours (14.3 days) at the worst point in the zone.
- **With the two relays:** about 98% from every point in the zone; longest gap 2 hours.

## Against NASA's sunlight map

The #1 target is number 2 of the ten on NASA's average-sunlight map. The target that map rates best needs 33.4 t, so ranking by kit weight saves 0.2 t.
Across the region's ten targets the kit runs from 33.2 t to 39.9 t.

## Water ice nearby (information only)

The nearest permanently shadowed area of at least 1 km² is 9.7 km from the reference point (60.6 km²). At its hottest it reaches 77 K; 100% of it is cold enough for ice at the surface. It is too far to walk (the on-foot reach is 300 m), and it never changes the kit or a ranking.

## What stands out

- Its kit ranks 8 of the 9 regions by weight (33.2 t; 1 is lightest).
- The lowest direct Earth contact of the nine (47% at the target); the relays matter most here.
- The panel spot is 197 m from the target, at the edge of the panel search.

## Limits

- Numbers are for one case: the crewed mission's year-round base over 2027, panels and antenna at 10 m. Other dates and stays come from the app's own runs.
- Slope comes from 20 m pixels, so boulders and small craters are invisible.
- The ten targets are shortlisted by NASA's average-sunlight map before the full calculation, so a target with a shorter night but lower average could be missed.
