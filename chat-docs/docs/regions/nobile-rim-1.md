# Nobile Rim 1

Nobile Rim 1 is one of NASA's nine Artemis III candidate landing regions near the Moon's south pole. On the rim of Nobile crater, named for the Italian polar explorer Umberto Nobile.

## Where it is

- **Size:** about 400 km².
- **Reference point** (its sunniest pixel on NASA's sunlight map): 85.30°S, 37.10°E, about 143 km from the pole.
- **Sunlight at the reference point:** 83% on NASA's long-term average map (60 m pixels).
- **Landing zones that pass the slope rule:** 12% of those tried (at least 95% of a landing zone of 100 m radius under 10°).

## The #1 landing target (year-round crewed base, 2027)

The target that needs the lightest power kit. The site card in the app shows it. By kit weight it ranks 2 of the 9 regions (1 is lightest).

- **Where:** 85.54°S, 37.63°E, 7.5 km from the reference point.
- **Landing zone:** 100% under 10°; average slope 4.5°, steepest pixel 6.8°.
- **Panel spot:** 102 m from the target, slope 0.8°, on a 10 m mast.
- **Sunlight at the panels:** 82% of the year; longest night 9.3 days (224 hours).
- **At the landing point itself:** 83% sunlight, longest night 9.3 days.
- **Power kit:** 22.1 t: battery 21.4 t (3,216 kWh), solar array 0.7 t (148 m²).
- **Direct Earth contact:** 65% at the target (64%–66% across the zone); longest direct blackout 234 hours (9.8 days) at the worst point in the zone.
- **With the two relays:** about 98% from every point in the zone; longest gap 2 hours.

## Against NASA's sunlight map

The #1 target is also the one NASA's average-sunlight map rates best, so ranking by kit weight and by sunlight pick the same spot here.
Across the region's ten targets the kit runs from 22.1 t to 31.1 t.

## Water ice nearby (information only)

The nearest permanently shadowed area of at least 1 km² is 5.0 km from the reference point (1.0 km²). At its hottest it reaches 180 K; 29% of it is cold enough for ice at the surface. It is too far to walk (the on-foot reach is 300 m), and it never changes the kit or a ranking.

## What stands out

- Its kit ranks 2 of the 9 regions by weight (22.1 t; 1 is lightest).
- The sunniest panels of the nine (82% of the year).
- Its nearest shadowed area is the closest of the nine (5.0 km).

## Limits

- Numbers are for one case: the crewed mission's year-round base over 2027, panels and antenna at 10 m. Other dates and stays come from the app's own runs.
- Slope comes from 20 m pixels, so boulders and small craters are invisible.
- The ten targets are shortlisted by NASA's average-sunlight map before the full calculation, so a target with a shorter night but lower average could be missed.
