# Malapert Massif

Malapert Massif is one of NASA's nine Artemis III candidate landing regions near the Moon's south pole. A mountain massif named after the nearby crater Malapert, itself named for the 17th-century astronomer Charles Malapert.

## Where it is

- **Size:** about 441 km².
- **Reference point** (its sunniest pixel on NASA's sunlight map): 86.00°S, 2.06°E, about 121 km from the pole.
- **Sunlight at the reference point:** 82% on NASA's long-term average map (60 m pixels).
- **Landing zones that pass the slope rule:** 7% of those tried (at least 95% of a landing zone of 100 m radius under 10°).

## The #1 landing target (year-round crewed base, 2027)

The target that needs the lightest power kit. The site card in the app shows it. By kit weight it ranks 5 of the 9 regions (1 is lightest).

- **Where:** 86.03°S, 3.07°E, 2.4 km from the reference point.
- **Landing zone:** 100% under 10°; average slope 1.5°, steepest pixel 9.7°.
- **Panel spot:** 189 m from the target, slope 2.2°, on a 10 m mast.
- **Sunlight at the panels:** 79% of the year; longest night 7.2 days (173 hours).
- **At the landing point itself:** 79% sunlight, longest night 7.8 days.
- **Power kit:** 25.3 t: battery 24.2 t (3,629 kWh), solar array 1.1 t (256 m²).
- **Direct Earth contact:** 66% at the target (59%–69% across the zone); longest direct blackout 269 hours (11.2 days) at the worst point in the zone.
- **With the two relays:** about 98% from every point in the zone; longest gap 2 hours.

## Against NASA's sunlight map

The #1 target is number 5 of the ten on NASA's average-sunlight map. The target that map rates best needs 26.7 t, so ranking by kit weight saves 1.4 t.
Across the region's ten targets the kit runs from 25.3 t to 32.9 t.

## Water ice nearby (information only)

The nearest permanently shadowed area of at least 1 km² is 10.2 km from the reference point (1.4 km²). At its hottest it reaches 159 K; 27% of it is cold enough for ice at the surface. It is too far to walk (the on-foot reach is 300 m), and it never changes the kit or a ranking.

## What stands out

- Its kit ranks 5 of the 9 regions by weight (25.3 t; 1 is lightest).

## Limits

- Numbers are for one case: the crewed mission's year-round base over 2027, panels and antenna at 10 m. Other dates and stays come from the app's own runs.
- Slope comes from 20 m pixels, so boulders and small craters are invisible.
- The ten targets are shortlisted by NASA's average-sunlight map before the full calculation, so a target with a shorter night but lower average could be missed.
