# Mons Mouton Plateau

Mons Mouton Plateau is one of NASA's nine Artemis III candidate landing regions near the Moon's south pole. A plateau next to the Mons Mouton mountain. By far the largest of the nine regions.

## Where it is

- **Size:** about 4452 km².
- **Reference point** (its sunniest pixel on NASA's sunlight map): 85.48°S, 29.52°E, about 137 km from the pole.
- **Sunlight at the reference point:** 78% on NASA's long-term average map (120 m pixels).
- **Landing zones that pass the slope rule:** 29% of those tried (at least 95% of a landing zone of 100 m radius under 10°).

## The #1 landing target (year-round crewed base, 2027)

The target that needs the lightest power kit. The site card in the app shows it. By kit weight it ranks 3 of the 9 regions (1 is lightest).

- **Where:** 84.67°S, 39.49°E, 35.6 km from the reference point.
- **Landing zone:** 100% under 10°; average slope 3.7°, steepest pixel 8.5°.
- **Panel spot:** 200 m from the target, slope 2.4°, on a 10 m mast.
- **Sunlight at the panels:** 77% of the year; longest night 9.3 days (224 hours).
- **At the landing point itself:** 75% sunlight, longest night 11.2 days.
- **Power kit:** 22.5 t: battery 22.2 t (3,328 kWh), solar array 0.3 t (67 m²).
- **Direct Earth contact:** 98% at the target (76%–98% across the zone); longest direct blackout 155 hours (6.5 days) at the worst point in the zone.
- **With the two relays:** about 98% from every point in the zone; longest gap 2 hours.

## Against NASA's sunlight map

The #1 target is number 8 of the ten on NASA's average-sunlight map. The target that map rates best needs 30.6 t, so ranking by kit weight saves 8.1 t.
Across the region's ten targets the kit runs from 22.5 t to 30.6 t.

## Water ice nearby (information only)

The nearest permanently shadowed area of at least 1 km² is 11.9 km from the reference point (6.3 km²). At its hottest it reaches 80 K; 67% of it is cold enough for ice at the surface. It is too far to walk (the on-foot reach is 300 m), and it never changes the kit or a ranking.

## What stands out

- Its kit ranks 3 of the 9 regions by weight (22.5 t; 1 is lightest).
- The best direct Earth contact of the nine (98% at the target).
- The panel spot is 200 m from the target, at the edge of the panel search.
- The region only fits NASA's coarser 120 m sunlight map, so its reference point is less precise.

## Limits

- Numbers are for one case: the crewed mission's year-round base over 2027, panels and antenna at 10 m. Other dates and stays come from the app's own runs.
- Slope comes from 20 m pixels, so boulders and small craters are invisible.
- The ten targets are shortlisted by NASA's average-sunlight map before the full calculation, so a target with a shorter night but lower average could be missed.
