# de Gerlache Rim 2

de Gerlache Rim 2 is one of NASA's nine Artemis III candidate landing regions near the Moon's south pole. On the rim of de Gerlache crater, named for the Belgian polar explorer Adrien de Gerlache.

## Where it is

- **Size:** about 400 km².
- **Reference point** (its sunniest pixel on NASA's sunlight map): 88.47°S, 67.85°W, about 47 km from the pole.
- **Sunlight at the reference point:** 75% on NASA's long-term average map (60 m pixels).
- **Landing zones that pass the slope rule:** 19% of those tried (at least 95% of a landing zone of 100 m radius under 10°).

## The #1 landing target (year-round crewed base, 2027)

The target that needs the lightest power kit. The site card in the app shows it. By kit weight it ranks 1 of the 9 regions (1 is lightest).

- **Where:** 88.59°S, 67.91°W, 3.7 km from the reference point.
- **Landing zone:** 100% under 10°; average slope 1.7°, steepest pixel 7.0°.
- **Panel spot:** 198 m from the target, slope 4.4°, on a 10 m mast.
- **Sunlight at the panels:** 75% of the year; longest night 6.8 days (163 hours).
- **At the landing point itself:** 81% sunlight, longest night 6.8 days.
- **Power kit:** 18.9 t: battery 17.8 t (2,667 kWh), solar array 1.1 t (268 m²).
- **Direct Earth contact:** 59% at the target (59%–59% across the zone); longest direct blackout 274 hours (11.4 days) at the worst point in the zone.
- **With the two relays:** about 98% from every point in the zone; longest gap 2 hours.

## Against NASA's sunlight map

The #1 target is number 2 of the ten on NASA's average-sunlight map. The target that map rates best needs 29.0 t, so ranking by kit weight saves 10.1 t.
Across the region's ten targets the kit runs from 18.9 t to 41.7 t.

## Water ice nearby (information only)

The nearest permanently shadowed area of at least 1 km² is 6.7 km from the reference point (3.5 km²). At its hottest it reaches 141 K; 45% of it is cold enough for ice at the surface. It is too far to walk (the on-foot reach is 300 m), and it never changes the kit or a ranking.

## What stands out

- The lightest power kit of the nine (18.9 t).
- The shortest night of the nine at its panels (6.8 days).
- The closest to the pole (47 km).
- The panel spot is 198 m from the target, at the edge of the panel search.

## Limits

- Numbers are for one case: the crewed mission's year-round base over 2027, panels and antenna at 10 m. Other dates and stays come from the app's own runs.
- Slope comes from 20 m pixels, so boulders and small craters are invisible.
- The ten targets are shortlisted by NASA's average-sunlight map before the full calculation, so a target with a shorter night but lower average could be missed.
