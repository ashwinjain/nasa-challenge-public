# Mons Mouton

Mons Mouton is one of NASA's nine Artemis III candidate landing regions near the Moon's south pole. A mountain (mons) named after Melba Roy Mouton, a NASA mathematician and programmer.

## Where it is

- **Size:** about 256 km².
- **Reference point** (its sunniest pixel on NASA's sunlight map): 85.44°S, 32.20°E, about 138 km from the pole.
- **Sunlight at the reference point:** 75% on NASA's long-term average map (60 m pixels).
- **Landing zones that pass the slope rule:** 24% of those tried (at least 95% of a landing zone of 100 m radius under 10°).

## The #1 landing target (year-round crewed base, 2027)

The target that needs the lightest power kit. The site card in the app shows it. By kit weight it ranks 4 of the 9 regions (1 is lightest).

- **Where:** 85.48°S, 31.36°E, 2.3 km from the reference point.
- **Landing zone:** 99% under 10°; average slope 5.8°, steepest pixel 10.3°.
- **Panel spot:** 60 m from the target, slope 3.9°, on a 10 m mast.
- **Sunlight at the panels:** 80% of the year; longest night 9.4 days (226 hours).
- **At the landing point itself:** 80% sunlight, longest night 9.4 days.
- **Power kit:** 22.5 t: battery 21.4 t (3,208 kWh), solar array 1.1 t (256 m²).
- **Direct Earth contact:** 74% at the target (64%–77% across the zone); longest direct blackout 238 hours (9.9 days) at the worst point in the zone.
- **With the two relays:** about 98% from every point in the zone; longest gap 2 hours.

## Against NASA's sunlight map

The #1 target is number 3 of the ten on NASA's average-sunlight map. The target that map rates best needs 24.9 t, so ranking by kit weight saves 2.4 t.
Across the region's ten targets the kit runs from 22.5 t to 40.4 t.

## Water ice nearby (information only)

The nearest permanently shadowed area of at least 1 km² is 6.0 km from the reference point (6.3 km²). At its hottest it reaches 80 K; 67% of it is cold enough for ice at the surface. It is too far to walk (the on-foot reach is 300 m), and it never changes the kit or a ranking.

## What stands out

- Its kit ranks 4 of the 9 regions by weight (22.5 t; 1 is lightest).

## Limits

- Numbers are for one case: the crewed mission's year-round base over 2027, panels and antenna at 10 m. Other dates and stays come from the app's own runs.
- Slope comes from 20 m pixels, so boulders and small craters are invisible.
- The ten targets are shortlisted by NASA's average-sunlight map before the full calculation, so a target with a shorter night but lower average could be missed.
