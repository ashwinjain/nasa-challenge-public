# The nine Artemis III regions at a glance

In October 2024 NASA named nine candidate landing regions near the Moon's
south pole for the first crewed Artemis landing. The navigator covers only
these nine. Each has its own page:

- [Haworth](haworth.md)
- [Malapert Massif](malapert-massif.md)

## How to read these pages

- **Reference point:** the sunniest pixel in the region on NASA's average
  sunlight map. The app's timeline, tiles and 3D view use it.
- **#1 landing target:** the safe landing target that needs the lightest
  power kit. The site card shows it.
- **Numbers are for one fixed case:** the crewed mission's year-round base
  over 2027, panels and antenna on a 10 m mast. For other dates or stays,
  the app (and the chat's data tools) work the numbers out fresh.
- **Distance from the pole** is measured along the surface from the
  reference point.

## Side by side (#1 target, year-round crewed base, 2027)

| Region | From the pole | Power kit | Longest night at the panels | Sunlight at the panels | Direct Earth contact at the target |

| Nobile Rim 1 | 143 km | 22.1 t | 9.3 days | 82% | 65% |
| Mons Mouton Plateau | 137 km | 22.5 t | 9.3 days | 77% | 98% |
| Mons Mouton | 138 km | 22.5 t | 9.4 days | 80% | 74% |
| Malapert Massif | 121 km | 25.3 t | 7.2 days | 79% | 66% |
| Peak Near Cabeus B | 194 km | 29.1 t | 12.1 days | 66% | 96% |
| Haworth | 110 km | 33.0 t | 13.7 days | 62% | 81% |
| Slater Plain | 83 km | 33.2 t | 14.3 days | 66% | 47% |
| Nobile Rim 2 | 190 km | 36.0 t | 15.2 days | 52% | 63% |

With the two relay satellites, contact is about 98% at every target. The
battery is most of every kit.

## What sets them apart

- **Lightest kits:** de Gerlache Rim 2 (18.9 t), then Nobile Rim 1 and Mons Mouton Plateau (22.1 t and 22.5 t).
- **Shortest night:** de Gerlache Rim 2 (6.8 days); its kit ranks 1 of 9.
- **Best direct Earth contact:** Mons Mouton Plateau and Peak Near Cabeus B, near the most the Deep Space Network allows (about 98%).
- **Heaviest kits:** Nobile Rim 2, Slater Plain and Haworth, where nights at the panels last about two weeks.
- **Most rugged:** Peak Near Cabeus B and Malapert Massif, where only 3% and 7% of the landing zones tried pass the slope rule.
