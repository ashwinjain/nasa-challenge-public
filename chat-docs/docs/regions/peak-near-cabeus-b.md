# Peak Near Cabeus B

Peak Near Cabeus B is one of NASA's nine Artemis III candidate landing regions near the Moon's south pole. A peak near Cabeus B, a smaller crater named after the large crater Cabeus (after the 17th-century scientist Niccolò Cabeo).

## Where it is

- **Size:** about 400 km².
- **Reference point** (its sunniest pixel on NASA's sunlight map): 83.62°S, 68.59°W, about 194 km from the pole.
- **Sunlight at the reference point:** 68% on NASA's long-term average map (120 m pixels).
- **Landing zones that pass the slope rule:** 3% of those tried (at least 95% of a landing zone of 100 m radius under 10°).

## The #1 landing target (year-round crewed base, 2027)

The target that needs the lightest power kit. The site card in the app shows it. By kit weight it ranks 6 of the 9 regions (1 is lightest).

- **Where:** 83.58°S, 68.44°W, 1.1 km from the reference point.
- **Landing zone:** 99% under 10°; average slope 2.7°, steepest pixel 10.1°.
- **Panel spot:** 198 m from the target, slope 2.6°, on a 10 m mast.
- **Sunlight at the panels:** 66% of the year; longest night 12.1 days (290 hours).
- **At the landing point itself:** 65% sunlight, longest night 12.7 days.
- **Power kit:** 29.1 t: battery 28.8 t (4,320 kWh), solar array 0.3 t (78 m²).
- **Direct Earth contact:** 96% at the target (96%–96% across the zone); longest direct blackout 47 hours (2.0 days) at the worst point in the zone.
- **With the two relays:** about 98% from every point in the zone; longest gap 2 hours.

## Against NASA's sunlight map

The #1 target is number 2 of the ten on NASA's average-sunlight map. The target that map rates best needs 29.2 t, so ranking by kit weight saves 0.1 t.
Across the region's ten targets the kit runs from 29.1 t to 33.8 t.

## Water ice nearby (information only)

The nearest permanently shadowed area of at least 1 km² is 13.5 km from the reference point (2.2 km²). At its hottest it reaches 221 K; 0% of it is cold enough for ice at the surface. It is too far to walk (the on-foot reach is 300 m), and it never changes the kit or a ranking.

## What stands out

- Its kit ranks 6 of the 9 regions by weight (29.1 t; 1 is lightest).
- The most rugged: only 3% of the landing zones tried pass the slope rule.
- The farthest from the pole (194 km).
- Its nearest shadowed area is never cold enough for ice at the surface (221 K at its hottest); ice could last about 0.24 m down.
- The panel spot is 198 m from the target, at the edge of the panel search.
- The region only fits NASA's coarser 120 m sunlight map, so its reference point is less precise.

## Limits

- Numbers are for one case: the crewed mission's year-round base over 2027, panels and antenna at 10 m. Other dates and stays come from the app's own runs.
- Slope comes from 20 m pixels, so boulders and small craters are invisible.
- The ten targets are shortlisted by NASA's average-sunlight map before the full calculation, so a target with a shorter night but lower average could be missed.
