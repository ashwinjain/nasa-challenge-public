# Haworth

Haworth is one of NASA's nine Artemis III candidate landing regions near the Moon's south pole. The region takes its name from Haworth crater nearby.

## Where it is

- **Size:** about 888 km².
- **Reference point** (its sunniest pixel on NASA's sunlight map): 86.37°S, 23.28°W, about 110 km from the pole.
- **Sunlight at the reference point:** 70% on NASA's long-term average map (60 m pixels).
- **Landing zones that pass the slope rule:** 26% of those tried (at least 95% of a landing zone of 100 m radius under 10°).

## The #1 landing target (year-round crewed base, 2027)

The target that needs the lightest power kit. The site card in the app shows it. By kit weight it ranks 7 of the 9 regions (1 is lightest).

- **Where:** 86.35°S, 23.21°W, 0.6 km from the reference point.
- **Landing zone:** 100% under 10°; average slope 4.8°, steepest pixel 7.8°.
- **Panel spot:** 200 m from the target, slope 0.7°, on a 10 m mast.
- **Sunlight at the panels:** 62% of the year; longest night 13.7 days (328 hours).
- **At the landing point itself:** 61% sunlight, longest night 14.0 days.
- **Power kit:** 33.0 t: battery 32.6 t (4,892 kWh), solar array 0.4 t (82 m²).
- **Direct Earth contact:** 81% at the target (75%–81% across the zone); longest direct blackout 168 hours (7.0 days) at the worst point in the zone.
- **With the two relays:** about 98% from every point in the zone; longest gap 2 hours.

## Against NASA's sunlight map

The #1 target is number 7 of the ten on NASA's average-sunlight map. The target that map rates best needs 33.0 t, so ranking by kit weight saves 0.0 t.
Across the region's ten targets the kit runs from 33.0 t to 51.8 t.

## Water ice nearby (information only)

The nearest permanently shadowed area of at least 1 km² is 9.4 km from the reference point (3.6 km²). At its hottest it reaches 73 K; 76% of it is cold enough for ice at the surface. It is too far to walk (the on-foot reach is 300 m), and it never changes the kit or a ranking.

## What stands out

- Its kit ranks 7 of the 9 regions by weight (33.0 t; 1 is lightest).
- Its nearest shadowed area is the coldest of the nine (73 K at its hottest).
- The panel spot is 200 m from the target, at the edge of the panel search.

## Limits

- Numbers are for one case: the crewed mission's year-round base over 2027, panels and antenna at 10 m. Other dates and stays come from the app's own runs.
- Slope comes from 20 m pixels, so boulders and small craters are invisible.
- The ten targets are shortlisted by NASA's average-sunlight map before the full calculation, so a target with a shorter night but lower average could be missed.
